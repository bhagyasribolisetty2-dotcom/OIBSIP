Scene III — Karnal, Haryana

Where the Story Begins

Birthplace

Karnal, a town in the north Indian state of Haryana, about 125 km north of New Delhi.

Date of Birth

NASA's official biography lists 1 July 1961. Her family and many Indian sources give 17 March 1962, explaining that the official date was recorded so she could sit her school matriculation exam early.

Early Years

She grew up the youngest of four children and attended Tagore Bal Niketan Senior Secondary School in Karnal, where she was drawn to the aircraft of the local flying club.

Because records conflict, both birth dates are shown. Her birth hospital is not named here because no reliable source documents it.

Scene IV — Childhood & Family

Where Imagination Began

In school she chose her own name, Kalpana, which means “imagination”. At home she was affectionately called Montu. Watching aeroplanes from the Karnal flying club, she would ask her father to take her for joyrides, and she drew aircraft in school when other children drew landscapes.

Banarasi Lal Chawla

Her father, a businessman in Karnal. He encouraged her curiosity and her early fascination with flight.

Sanjyothi Chawla

Her mother, who supported her decision to pursue engineering at a time when few women entered the field.

KC

Siblings

Kalpana was the youngest of four children: sisters Sunita and Deepa, and brother Sanjay.

JP

Jean-Pierre Harrison

She married Jean-Pierre Harrison, a flying instructor and aviation author, in 1983.

  8. 1988

    Moffett Field, California

Scene VI — Space Missions

Two Flights Aboard Columbia

STS-87

Launched 19 Nov 1997 Space Shuttle Columbia 15 d 16 h 34 m
Objectives
The fourth U.S. Microgravity Payload (USMP-4) and studies of the Sun's outer atmosphere with the Spartan 201 satellite.
Her role
Mission specialist and prime robotic arm operator, responsible for deploying Spartan.
Achievements
252 orbits and 6.5 million miles in 376 hours, 34 minutes. The first woman of Indian origin to fly in space.

STS-107

Launched 16 Jan 2003 Space Shuttle Columbia 15 d 22 h 20 m
Objectives
A dedicated science and research mission using the SPACEHAB Research Double Module, with work around the clock in two shifts.
Her role
Flight engineer and mission specialist, supporting experiments in microgravity, life sciences and Earth observation.
Achievements
The crew completed about 80 experiments. Columbia was lost during re-entry on 1 February 2003, 16 minutes before its scheduled landing.

Scene IX

Her Legacy

Kalpana-1 Satellite

ISRO's meteorological satellite MetSat-1, launched in September 2002, was renamed Kalpana-1 in her honour in February 2003.

Honours & Awards

Posthumously awarded the Congressional Space Medal of Honor, the NASA Space Flight Medal and the NASA Distinguished Service Medal.

Institutions

Kalpana Chawla Government Medical College in Karnal and Kalpana Chawla Hall at the University of Texas at Arlington carry her name.

Women in Science

The Government of Karnataka's Kalpana Chawla Award recognises young women scientists, and scholarships in her name support engineering students.

Among the Stars

Asteroid 51826 Kalpanachawla and Northrop Grumman's Cygnus NG-14 spacecraft, the “S.S. Kalpana Chawla” (2020), bear her name.

Future Astronauts

Her journey from a small town to orbit continues to inspire students across India and the world to pursue aerospace careers.
